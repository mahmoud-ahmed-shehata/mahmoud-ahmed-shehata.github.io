Challenge

The situation on site

Joined the project 4 years behind schedule. The immediate challenge was not just execution — it was understanding exactly what remained, what was approved, what was stuck and what had been verbally agreed but never formally confirmed. Some materials had been "approved" verbally for years and were now disputed. Some required formal re-approval. Some had been installed and needed to be demolished and rebuilt on the owner's instruction because no formal approval existed. On top of this, the critical municipal approval for a post-tension roof extension was outstanding — blocking a large portion of the remaining structural scope. The project needed a full reset: a clear picture of every remaining work item, every pending approval, every stalled delivery and every live risk — before any meaningful recovery could begin.
Role & Actions

What I specifically did

01 Full scope inventory on day one — remaining works, pending approvals, stalled material deliveries, contractor disputes, and active live risks. That picture determined the recovery sequence.
02 Resolved the critical municipal approval for the post-tension roof extension — the single biggest blocker on the structural scope. Cleared this to unlock progress on the roof and remaining structure.
03 Worked through disputed material approvals — identifying which could be formally re-approved, which required workarounds to avoid time loss, and which had to be demolished and rebuilt per owner instruction.
04 Implemented zone-based execution planning — running Zone A and Zone B works in parallel to maximise daily progress across finishes, MEP interfaces and specialist trades without congestion.
05 Supervised daily high-end finishing works room by room — marble, stone, porcelain, gypsum, wood panels and architectural details — preparing snag lists and closing items before client visits.
06 Coordinated external stone and aluminium cladding — anchoring details, waterproofing interfaces and façade alignment — ensuring correct fixing methods and clean interfaces with structure.
07 Managed RFIs and technical clarifications with the consultant for special details, finishes and structural modifications — keeping the approval chain moving rather than waiting on outstanding items.
08 Maintained safety and area protection across a live finishing environment — protecting completed rooms from ongoing works and coordinating MEP teams to prevent clashes with ceilings and decorative elements.
